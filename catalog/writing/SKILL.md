# Clear Writing

The reader is busy and does not owe you their attention. Earn it by being
shorter, plainer and more direct. Clarity is a courtesy, not a style choice.

## Rewrite rules, in order of impact

1. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence says what happened or what you
   want. Background comes after, if at all.
2. **Cut the throat-clearing.** Delete "I wanted to reach out", "just a quick
   note", "as you may be aware", "I hope this finds you well". Every sentence
   that existed only to warm up is waste.
3. **Shorten every sentence.** If it needs three clauses, it is three sentences.
   Target under 20 words; it is remarkable how much survives the cut.
4. **Use plain words.** "use" not "utilize", "before" not "prior to", "about"
   not "regarding", "try" not "attempt to". Fancy words are not precision; they
   are friction.
5. **Prefer the active voice.** "We fixed it" not "it was fixed". The passive
   voice hides who did what, and usually that is the one thing the reader needs.
6. **Cut weasel words.** "very", "really", "basically", "actually", "kind of",
   "somewhat", "in order to". Each one is a confession of doubt.
7. **One idea per paragraph.** A paragraph is a single step, not a bag.

## Match the medium

- **Email** - subject says the ask; body has the ask in the first line, then the
  why, then the next step. Sign off once, not three times.
- **Documentation** - a line for what it does, then the shortest example that
  works, then the caveats. Nobody reads documentation before they need it, so
  write it for someone who is mid-task.
- **Release note** - what changed and who it affects, before anything else. A
  breaking change goes first and in bold.
- **Explanation** - use an example before a definition. Concrete before abstract,
  every time.

## What to avoid

No exclamation marks for enthusiasm, no ALL CAPS for emphasis, no emoji as
punctuation, no "hope" as a closing strategy. If the news is bad, say it plainly
in the first line - burying it makes it worse, not kinder.

## Output

Give the rewritten text. Then, in one line, say what you cut and why. Do not
reproduce the original at length, and do not add a preamble praising the
rewrite.
